# Glossary

Horatio's words, in plain English, in alphabetical order. Each has its own heading, so other pages can link straight to it.

## Accepted character

A character a troupe has accepted as its [home troupe](#home-troupe). Until then, its player can edit it freely, and it takes part in no downtimes.

## Accepted sheet

The character's sheet as its home troupe accepted it. The player can't lower a rating below it; only storytellers take accepted dots away.

## Approval

A decision by the home troupe's storytellers or assistant storytellers on a change that waits for them: a character asking to make the troupe its home, a discipline purchase, a merit or flaw change, an [exception](#exception) or a backstory. Most other changes to a sheet take effect at once.

## Assistant storyteller

A member of a troupe's [staff](#staff) who can do everything a storyteller can, except manage the staff, the house rules and the connectors, and edit the troupe's profile.

## Backstory

A character's past, written by its player in Markdown, either freely or by answering the troupe's backstory questions. Only its player, the custodian and the home troupe's storytellers and assistant storytellers read it. A troupe can require it approved before it accepts the character.

## Cart

The changes to a sheet a player has gathered on the Sheet tab but not yet made: raises, lowers, new traits and swaps. **Spend XP** makes them all at once, or none if any is refused. The cart is kept in your browser, one per character.

## Character

A persona a player plays. It belongs to its player, plays in one [home troupe](#home-troupe), and can visit others. A character is never deleted, only [retired](#retired-character).

## Check

A rule about what a sheet may hold, rather than what it costs, such as a merit only one clan may take, or two flaws that can't go together. A change that breaks a check is refused, with the reason and the book's page.

## Check-in

How players say they're at a game. Staff open check-in for the night, and players check in from the troupe's page or by its code, landing in their [Play View](#play-view). Staff then see who came, and award the game's XP to everyone there at once.

## Connector

A way out of Horatio that a troupe sets up, such as its Discord server or email, for sending downtime results. Results always arrive in Horatio too.

## Custodian

The person who looks after the whole site, above every troupe. The custodian appoints lead storytellers, looks after people's accounts and the system, and can do anything in any troupe.

## Customise

The troupe's tab group where its storytellers set it up: its settings, its rules and [house rules](#house-rule), specialisations and connectors.

## Delivery

Sending downtime responses to their players. By default only submissions with every response written go out; staff can choose to send partly written ones too.

## Discipline purchase

A discipline dot bought once a character has a home troupe. The XP is spent at once, and the dot shows as awaiting approval until the home troupe decides. Declining it takes the dot back and refunds the XP.

## Downtime

What a character does between games: the player fills in the troupe's form, and staff answer each action with a response.

## Downtime cycle

One round of downtimes for a troupe, with the date it opens, the date downtimes are due, and a grace period for late ones. Often just called a cycle.

## Equipment

The items a character holds: weapons, protective gear, other gear and Vampiric Texts. Only the home troupe's storytellers and assistant storytellers give, change or take items. At a game, a player equips items on the Play View, and their qualities change the test pools.

## Exception

A trait a character holds despite the rules, or at no XP cost, or both: a plot reward or a storyteller's ruling. A player asks for one in the cart, saying why, and it waits for approval; or staff give one directly, always with a reason.

## Free changes

A while after a troupe accepts a new character, if the troupe allows it, its player can still drop or lower traits for their full XP back, as during creation. Each troupe chooses none, a number of days, or a number of games.

## Free rating

The part of a rating the character didn't pay XP for: from creation, or given by another trait.

## Game

One of a troupe's game nights. The troupe's schedule lists the games to come, members say whether they're coming, and players [check in](#check-in) on the night.

## Handle

The unique name you're found and shown by, such as @night_owl, instead of your email. You can sign in with it, and change it once in 30 days.

## Home troupe

The one troupe that holds a character's sheet. It accepts the character, awards its XP, approves its changes, and runs the downtimes it takes part in. A character has at most one.

## Home troupe change

A player asking for a character to be given a home troupe, or to move to a new one. Any differences between the character and the new troupe's rules are settled one by one ([reconciliation](#reconciliation)) before the new troupe accepts it.

## House merit

A merit or flaw a troupe makes itself, marked ★ wherever it shows. Only the troupe's own characters can hold one.

## House rule

A troupe's change to the book's rules: a different cost or starting allowance, a check of its own, a ban, its own wording, and so on. A character plays by its home troupe's house rules. The troupe's **House rules** tab lists them for everyone.

## Lead storyteller

The troupe's one owner: a storyteller who can also rename the troupe, edit its description and archive it. Every troupe has exactly one.

## Lines and veils

A player's content boundaries, in their own words. A **line** is something that must not happen in play around them; a **veil** may happen, but off-screen. The staff of their troupes always see them; other players only if the player shares them.

## Merit or flaw change

Adding or removing a merit or flaw once a character has a home troupe. The player asks, and nothing changes on the sheet until the home troupe approves.

## Narrator

A member of a troupe's [staff](#staff) who can do whatever a player there can, and also answer downtime actions given to them. A narrator never sees character sheets.

## Notification

Something in your bell. A task is work waiting on you, and goes once it's done; a message tells you something happened, and stays until you read it.

## NPC

A character the troupe's storytellers or assistant storytellers make and keep for the troupe. It needs no approval and takes no part in downtimes.

## Play View

The character's page for use at a game, made for a phone: trackers for Blood, Willpower and health that you tap as you spend them, test pools, equipment and status. It keeps working with poor signal and catches up when it can.

## Player

A person who plays one or more characters. A player sees only their own characters' sheets, never other players'.

## Player Nominations

Short notes players write in their downtimes praising someone's roleplaying. The troupe's storytellers read them, with their writers' names, then publish them each month to the troupe's players, shuffled and without names. A troupe chooses whether to use them.

## Profile

What anyone signed in can see about a person: their display name, handle, pronouns, when they joined, their bio, social media links and Discord username. Never their email.

## Quest

If a troupe uses them, a goal a character pursues between and at games, with requirements to meet and scenarios to pass. Staff build each one, and record how it goes.

## Rating

How many dots a character has in a [trait](#trait).

## Reconciliation

Settling, one by one, the differences between a character's sheet and the rules of a troupe it's joining, such as a trait that costs more there. A character isn't let in until every difference is settled.

## Retired character

A character its player has stopped playing: Final Death, Off-field or Inactive. It's hidden from everyday lists and out of downtimes, but kept with its full history.

## Scene

Several characters of one troupe on one page of the Play View, side by side, for staff running a scene.

## Staff

The people who run a troupe: its [lead storyteller](#lead-storyteller), [storytellers](#storyteller), [assistant storytellers](#assistant-storyteller) and [narrators](#narrator).

## Status

A character's standing in their sect and troupe, from their titles and status traits, shown on the character's **Status & Titles** tab and the troupe's **Status** tab.

## Storyteller

A member of a troupe's [staff](#staff) who runs it. Storytellers and the lead storyteller are the only ones who manage the troupe's staff and house rules, and edit its profile.

## Suspended account

Someone the custodian has shut out for a while. They're signed out, can't sign in, and get no notifications until the suspension ends. Their characters stay in their troupes.

## Title

A position a character holds in one troupe, such as Prince or Primogen. A title means nothing outside its troupe, and the active one gives status.

## Trait

One entry on a character's sheet, such as a skill, merit, flaw, discipline or background, usually with a [rating](#rating).

## Troupe

The group that runs games, with its own staff and house rules. Characters join it. Nothing sits above a troupe but the [custodian](#custodian).

## Troupe profile

What a troupe tells players and people thinking of joining: its picture, schedule, venue, setting, whether it takes new players, and content notes. A troupe can make it public, for anyone to read.

## Vault

All of a player's own characters, whether or not they're in a troupe.

## View as

The custodian seeing Horatio exactly as one person does, to help with a question: read-only, for at most 30 minutes, with a reason the person is shown.

## Visiting troupe

A troupe a character is in that isn't its home troupe. Its staff can see the sheet, but approve nothing on it and award it no XP.

## Who's Who

A troupe's list of its characters, for its staff and the players of characters in it: the court by title, each clan, and those no longer with us. It's never public.

## XP

Experience points, which buy traits. A character's **Experience** tab lists every award and purchase.

## XP debt

XP a character owes, put on it by its home troupe's staff or from its Embrace. All the XP it earns goes to the debt until it's paid off, and nothing that costs XP can be bought meanwhile.

## XP floor and ceiling

The least and the most XP a character in a troupe should have by now, from the troupe's starting XP, its games and its downtimes. A character joining below the floor is offered a top-up; one above the ceiling is shown to staff.
